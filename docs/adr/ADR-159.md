# ADR-159: AgentVet, a companion that vets the agent, on `sv`'s engine with a binary of its own, reporting what the record shows and deciding nothing

**Status:** accepted, 21 November 2026, in the pull request that built its first part (backlog 0705, part 1); proposed
the same day. Asked for by the owner on 21 November 2026, after the white paper's conclusion
that seeing what an agent did, against what it said, is the foundation for trusting what it built. Nothing here is
built. The owner chose the name (AgentVet, over AIVet, which reads as vetting AI apps, and `sv` already does that
through AISVS), the ConfigVet model (ADR-157), and the three answers under "The owner's answers". The design is the
design entry "AgentVet, a companion that vets the agent: what it did, what it said, and where the two part (21 November

record crate depends on (`crates/av-record/Cargo.toml`) and how it is laid out (`crates/av-record/src/lib.rs`); its
helpers (`crates/av-record/src/plain.rs`); the task envelope's reader (`crates/av-record/src/envelope.rs`,
`crates/av-record/src/envelope_tests.rs`); what is held to it (`crates/av-record/src/held.rs`,

`crates/av-record/src/checkpoint_tests.rs`); the note outside the folder (`crates/av-record/src/outside_note.rs`,
`crates/av-record/src/outside_note_tests.rs`, `crates/sv-cli/tests/note_outside.rs`); and the binary (`crates/av-cli/src/main.rs`, `crates/av-cli/Cargo.toml`,

## Context

`sv` grades an app. Three things it keeps are not about the app at all, but about the AI coding tool that built it:
the build-loop record of the tool's calls to `sv` (ADR-076), the hook record of the commands it ran and the files it
wrote (ADR-122), and the report's section on what the tool did that is worth a look (ADR-131). In the trials of 13 to
15 October 2026 these, read against each tool's transcript by hand, showed "the tests pass" with no test run, a finding
silenced by changing a value rather than fixing the code, and answers written in the owner's name. The white paper's
second lesson is that this visibility was the system's clearest contribution.

That work has outgrown `sv` in three ways. Its subject is the agent, not the app. It is continuous over a session,
where `sv` reads a folder at one moment. And its best evidence, the agent's own claims, is something `sv` deliberately
never reads (ADR-122, decision 2). The owner also wants it to cover any agent, not only one that writes code.

One weakness is known and must be said first. The security review of 21 November 2026 found, again and again, that a
recorder running as the same user as the agent can be rewritten by the agent: the hook record's chain is a plain
SHA-256 with no key, and `sv review`'s check for a terminal can be met by a program. A tool whose whole value is being
a witness has to say how independent a witness it is, for every line it shows.

## Decision

1. **One engine, one workspace, a third binary.** AgentVet is built from crates in this workspace and ships as a
   binary of its own, `av`, as ConfigVet ships as `cv` (ADR-157). It reuses the finding, credit, and tier types
   (`sv-core`), redaction (`Secret`), the text cleaning for a terminal and for one line (`sv_report::visible`,
   `sv_report::one_line`), the fence around untrusted text, the seal (ADR-034), and the exit codes (ADR-029). `sv`
   vets the app, `cv` the deployment, `av` the agent.
2. **The records move first, unchanged.** The build-loop record, the hook record, and the reader behind the report's
   section on the tool move from `sv-cli` into a shared crate, word for word, as the finding types moved for ConfigVet.
   `sv` reads them through that crate and its report says what it said before. What each record keeps does not change
   in that move (ADR-076, ADR-122, ADR-131 stand).
3. **Every piece of evidence carries the tier of its witness, strongest first:** recorded outside the agent's reach (a
   sandbox's network and process log, or a recorder under another account); recorded by the harness (hooks, telemetry
   the agent's runtime emits, a pass-through in front of its tools); derived from git and from the checkers' own
   records; and last, the agent's own word. A finding says which tier it rests on. Nothing the agent says raises a
   tier, and a gap in a record is itself reported, never read as "nothing happened".
4. **A claim has three verdicts, not two:** contradicted by the record, not supported by the record, or could not be
   checked. Only the first is a contradiction. A claim that could not be checked is not a lie, and is never counted as
   one.
5. **AgentVet decides nothing about the agent.** It never says an agent is trustworthy, aligned, or safe, as `sv`
   never says an app is secure. It says what the record shows, at which tier, and what it could not see.
6. **Rules first; a model's reading is the lowest tier and off by default.** The first detectors are rules over the
   record. A model may later read only the spans a rule flagged, fenced as data, and what it says is labeled a model's
   reading. It never produces a "contradicted" verdict on its own.
7. **The agent's words are read only with the owner's consent, for one session at a time.** A transcript is the only
   source of claims, and it holds the owner's prompts and may hold keys. `av` reads one only when the owner names it
   for that run, cuts keys as the secrets scan does, and keeps the claims it found and where, not the transcript. This
   is a new reading that ADR-122 does not allow `sv`; it is `av`'s alone.
8. **Drift is measured against what the owner declared.** A file the owner writes, the task envelope (`agentvet.toml`, a file of its own), says what the
   task is and what it may touch: folders, commands, hosts, tools, the files that guard oversight, and the actions
   that must ask first. Anything left out is a question in the report, never a pass. The envelope is the owner's
   word; one the agent wrote counts as the agent's, as a design answer does (ADR-022).
9. **Everything `av` reads is hostile text.** Transcripts, tool results, file names, and commit messages can be
   written by the agent or by whatever the agent read. Each is shown on one line or cleaned for the terminal, and
   fenced before any model reads it. The review of 21 November is the reason this is a decision and not a habit.
10. **`av` opens no network connection of its own.** Its pass-through relays what the agent already sends to a tool
    and adds nothing. Telemetry is read from a file or a local socket the owner points it at. A change here is a
    change to "The fence" in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and to this record.
11. **Report first. Stopping an action comes later, behind a decision of its own.** The first versions only report.
    Refusing or pausing an action is a different kind of power, and is built only after a Later entry here says which
    actions, on whose word, and what happens when `av` itself fails.
12. **The record is keyed, and its end is checkpointed under the owner's key.** (Changed on 22 November 2026, at the
    owner's word: the chain takes no key, and a note kept outside the app's folder does that work. See "As built,
    part 8". The checkpoints stand as written.) The chain that joins a record's lines
    takes a key, and its latest link is signed at intervals with the key `sv review` uses, so a rewrite after a
    checkpoint shows. This does not stop an agent that can read the key file; the report says so wherever it applies.
    A recorder under another account is the stronger step, and is not in the first build.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep it inside `sv`.** Rejected: `sv`'s report is about an app at a moment, its readers are not programmers, and
  ADR-122 keeps it from the agent's words for good reason. An agent that does not write code has no app to grade.
- **A separate project.** Rejected: "a finding went away with no fix" needs `sv`'s findings, and "the tests pass" needs
  the test run. The types, redaction, fence, and seal would be copied and drift apart.
- **A model as the judge from the start.** Rejected for the first build: its verdicts are not repeatable, it can be
  talked round by the text it judges, and it costs money on every session. Rules are free and say the same thing
  twice.
- **Stop actions from the first version.** Rejected for now, at the owner's word: a wrong refusal breaks the owner's
  work, and a refusal teaches the agent where the line is. Reporting comes first so the rules are proven on real
  sessions before any of them can say no.

## Consequences

- Three modules of `sv-cli` gain a second reader, so a change to what they keep is now a change for two programs.
- AgentVet will hold more sensitive text than `sv` ever has (decision 7). Its redaction, and where it keeps what, need
  the same care as a key.
- The honest limit in decision 12 will appear in every report until the stronger recorder is built.
- OWASP's Agentic Top 10 is a list of risks, not requirements, so a detector can name a related risk but cannot be
  credited against it the way a check is credited against ASVS. Which requirements of AISVS a detector speaks to is
  settled per detector, after reading the requirement's text.

## The owner's answers (21 November 2026)

1. **Who reads it first:** the owner, a solo builder who is a security engineer. Reports may show raw evidence and
   assume the reader knows what a hash chain is; plain language still holds.
2. **Report or stop:** "start with report, work towards stopping an action in certain circumstances."
3. **Which agents:** any agent, not only coding tools.
4. **Name and model:** AgentVet, following the ConfigVet model.

5. **A transcript is read only with consent for each session** (decision 7): "agree with your proposals".
6. **The first build's witness is the keyed, checkpointed chain alone** (decision 12), with the recorder under another
   account later: the same answer.
7. **The task envelope is a file of its own, `agentvet.toml`,** beside `stackvet.toml` and not a section of it: "use a
   separate agentvet.toml". So an agent with no app to grade needs no `stackvet.toml`, and `sv` never reads the
   envelope.

Answers 5 to 7 were given on 21 November 2026, after this record was first written. Nothing is left open; the record
stays proposed until the pull request that builds its first part makes it accepted.

## As built

Each part is added here by the pull request that builds it, as ADR-157's are.

### Part 1, 21 November 2026: the records move (decision 2; backlog 0705, part 1)

The build-loop record, the hook record, and the reader of what the tool did are in a crate of their own,
`crates/av-record`, with their tests. `sv-cli` gives each under its old path (`sv_cli::build_loop`,
`sv_cli::hook_record`, `sv_cli::tool_behavior`), and no test of `sv`'s changed what it expects.

Where the build differs from the plan:

- **The shared crate depends on `sv-check`.** The backlog item said `av` would sit on the shared crate and `sv-core`,
  "never on `sv-check`'s checks". The code does not allow that yet: the hook record cuts keys out of a command with
  the secrets scan's rules, and the reader of what the tool did reads git under ADR-032's settings, the bill of
  materials, and the reviews, all of which are in `sv-check`. So `av-record` depends on `sv-check`, as `cv-cli` does
  for the outside tools' runner (ADR-157, part 3). Moving redaction's rules and the git reader below `sv-check` would
  lift this, and is not done here.
- **Five small helpers moved with the records,** into `av_record::plain`, word for word: the SHA-256, the time
  written out, `Remedy` and `refuse_link`, and making a folder below another one level at a time. `sv-cli` reads each
  under its old name, so there is still one of each.
- **Two readers take a report's bytes, not its folder** (`hook_record::tip_in`, `tool_behavior::earlier_in`). Showing
  that `sv` wrote a report is the report seal's work, which stays in `sv-cli`; its wrappers do that first, as before.

### Part 2, 21 November 2026: `av report` (backlog 0705, part 2)

A binary of its own, `av`, in `crates/av-cli`, with one command: `av report <folder> [--hook-record] [--json]`.

- **What it reads.** The build-loop record, with the findings that went between the tool's first check and its last
  looked for again in the code (`build_loop::look_again`); git's history, through the reader of what the tool did;
  and the hook record, only for a run given `--hook-record` (ADR-122's consent, asked each time, kept as it is).
- **What it says.** For each record, what it shows and, in a sentence, how far it can be relied on (decision 3): each
  of the three is a file or a history the tool could rewrite, and the report says so every time. A finding that went
  with its line unchanged is listed; one whose line changed is not called a fix, since nothing read the change
  (decision 5).
- **What it does not look at, said every time** (decision 4's "could not be checked"): what the tool said against what
  it did, since no transcript is read yet (decision 7 is not built); whether the tool kept to its task, since there is
  no `agentvet.toml` yet (decision 8 is not built); and which findings a person set aside, which is `sv`'s report's to
  say.
- **It writes nothing and opens no connection** (decision 10). `crates/av-cli/tests/report.rs` holds that its own
  source names nothing that connects or starts a program and that its dependencies are the four it is meant to have;
  the crates beneath it read git as `sv` does (ADR-032).
- **Everything printed goes through `sv_report::visible`** (decision 9), held by a test that fails with it taken out.
- **Exit codes** follow ADR-029: 0 when the report was made, 2 when no record held anything about a tool, 3 when `av`
  failed.

Where the build differs from the plan: `av report` does not compare with `sv`'s last report, so it does not say
"fixed for real". Showing that `sv` wrote a report is the report seal's work, which is still in `sv-cli`; `sv report
--hook-record` already makes that comparison in its own section. And the keyed chain of decision 12 is not built: the
report's sentence on each record says what the plain chain does and does not show.

Not built: a transcript reader, the task envelope, the pass-through, any detector beyond what the records already
held, and anything that stops an action. `av` is not in the published image or a release.

### Part 3, 21 November 2026: the task envelope's format and its reader (decision 8; backlog 0715, part 1)

`agentvet.toml`, read by `crates/av-record/src/envelope.rs`, and said by `av report` in a section of its own. Nothing
is held to it yet: the detectors are the item's parts 2 and 3, and the report says so every time.

```toml
envelope-version = 1

[task]
goal = "Add a sign-out link to the header."

[may]
write = ["src/", "tests/"]        # a folder ends in a slash; anything else is one file
run = ["pytest", "npm"]           # the programs it may start, by name
reach = ["pypi.org"]              # the hosts it may connect to

[guards]
files = ["deploy/", "Makefile"]   # what it may not change, added to the list av always guards

ask-first = ["delete", "push"]    # of: delete, push, install, send, spend
```

- **A line left out is a question.** `[may] write`, `[may] run`, `[may] reach`, and `ask-first` left out are each asked
  in the report and assumed neither way; an empty list is the answer "nothing".
- **Read strictly.** A key or table the reader does not know is refused, never skipped, so a misspelled `[guards]`
  cannot pass as an envelope that guards nothing. A place that leaves the folder (`..`, a root, a drive), a version
  other than 1, an empty goal, and a file that is a link, is not an ordinary file, or is past 256 KB are refused too.
  A refused file holds nothing, and the report says why on one line.
- **Whose word it is.** The file does not say, so the report says every time that it may be the tool's own account,
  as an answer in `stackvet.toml` is the tool's until `sv review` records it (ADR-022). Recording the owner's word
  for an envelope is not built.
- **Every word of the file the report shows is on one line** (decision 9), held by a test with a goal that carries a
  line break and an escape character.

Choices made in the build, each small, each the owner's to change:

1. **No patterns.** An entry is a folder (ending in a slash, covering everything under it) or one file, compared as
   written; `./` is the whole folder. No `*`, and case is not folded. A pattern is easier to write too wide than a
   folder is.
2. **A program is named by one word** (`pytest`, not `pytest -q`). What arguments are allowed is not said.
3. **`ask-first` is one of five words:** delete, push, install, send, spend. Anything else is refused, so a word the
   detectors would not know cannot look like a rule.
4. **The guards that always hold,** whatever the file names: `agentvet.toml` itself; `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and
   `GEMINI.md`; the folders `.claude/`, `.cursor/`, `.windsurf/`, `.gemini/`, `.roo/`, and `.continue/`;
   `.cursorrules` and `.mcp.json`; and `.github/workflows/`, `.github/CODEOWNERS`, and `.gitlab-ci.yml`.
   `stackvet.toml` is not among them: the tool writes it by design. **`[guards] files` adds to these and takes none
   away: the owner's choice, 21 November 2026** ("make my own guard list add to the default"), over the first build,
   in which naming any list replaced them. So an envelope the tool wrote cannot unguard its own instruction files by
   naming a short list. The cost: there is no way yet to say one of these may be changed; a task that is about one
   of them will be listed as touching a guard, which is true, and for a person to read.
5. **`av report`'s exit code does not change with the envelope:** an envelope is not a record of the tool, so a folder
   with only an envelope still exits 2.

### Part 4, 21 November 2026: what the tool did, held to the envelope (decision 8; backlog 0715, part 2)

`crates/av-record/src/held.rs`, shown by `av report` under the task. Two of the design's groups: outside the envelope,
and tampering with oversight. It lists and decides nothing; a line is not a finding, and the report says many are
right to do.

- **What is read.** The files changed by commits whose message names a tool (ADR-131's reader), and, only for a run
  given `--hook-record`, the files the tool wrote and the commands it ran (ADR-122's consent, unchanged). Each line
  says which record it came from.
- **A guarded file changed** is listed whether or not `[may] write` covers it: being allowed to write somewhere is
  not being allowed to unguard it.
- **A file outside `[may] write`** is listed. A path that is not below the folder is outside it however it is
  written: one from a root or a drive, and one that climbs out with `..`, such as `src/../../home/x`, which begins
  like an allowed folder and is not in it.
- **A program `[may] run` does not name** is listed, read from a command's text: the first word of each part between
  `&&`, `||`, `;`, `|`, and `&`, by its name alone, past any `NAME=value` before it; a word that passes the next one
  on (`sudo`, `env`, `time`, `nohup`, `xargs`, `exec`) is listed, and so is what it starts.
- **What is not held, said every time** (decision 4): what a started program, a script, or a quoted command went on
  to do; the hosts reached, whatever `[may] reach` says, since no record read here shows them; whether the tool asked
  first, whatever `ask-first` says; anything `[may] write` or `[may] run` would cover when it is left out; files
  written and never committed, and every command, when the hook record was not asked for.
- **A commit's file names are set against the envelope only when the folder is the top of its repository.** Git names
  a file from that top, and the envelope from its own folder; for a folder further down the report says the history
  could not be held, rather than compare names that do not line up.

Held by `crates/av-record/src/held_tests.rs`, with a record that trips each thing looked for and one that does not,
and by `crates/av-cli/tests/report.rs`, end to end. With a climbing path taken as inside, with guarded files not
looked for, and with a person's commits read as the tool's, tests fail each time (one and one, two and two, and one).

Where it stops short: `[may] reach` and `ask-first` are read and said, and nothing can be held to them from these
records; that needs the pass-through (the design's next step). And a tool that commits without naming itself is not
seen through git at all, which is ADR-131's limit and is said in the report's sentence on git.

### Part 5, 21 November 2026: the six groups (backlog 0715, part 3)

`crates/av-record/src/groups.rs` gathers what the records show under the design's six groups, and `av report` ends
with them as a table, "By kind of thing". Nothing new is read but one thing: a tool's commits, for an entry written
in the owner's name.

| Group, as the report names it | What puts a line under it | From |
|---|---|---|
| What the tool said, against the record | A turn ended saying the app's tests pass, where the record shows otherwise | The hook record (ADR-122), when asked for |
| A check made to pass without a fix | A finding no longer found whose line reads as it did; a test deleted or skipped, an expectation changed, a scanner's settings edited, or a comment telling a check to look away | The build-loop record; git |
| Outside what the task file allows | Part 4's lines | Git; the hook record |
| Changes to what watches the tool | Part 4's guarded files; a record whose chain stops checking out; the build-loop record turned off | Git; both records |
| Written in your name | A tool's commit adding `by = "owner"` or a `seal` to `stackvet.toml`, or `Written by: owner` or `Sealed by sv review:` to `security-notes.md` or `design-decisions.md` | Git |
| Trying again after a no | Nothing yet | No record read shows a refusal |

- **A claim has a verdict** (decision 4). No test command before the claim, or a failed one, is *contradicted by the
  record*. A test command that finished without an error, with files written after it, is only *not supported by the
  record*: the tests may still pass. A claim that could not be checked is never a line.
- **An empty group says what it could not show, every time,** so it never reads as a clean one. "Trying again after a
  no" is always empty, and says that no record read shows what the tool was refused; what `sv` itself refused, for a
  tool it does not have, is said beside it as not a no from the owner.
- **`by` is read as a whole key,** so `standby = "owner"` and a commented-out line are not listed; a test found the
  first while this was built. Only a commit whose message names a tool is read: the owner recording their own word
  through `sv review` is not the tool's.
- **`av` does not check a seal.** It says a tool's commit added one, which only `sv review` at the owner's terminal
  makes; whether it holds is `sv report`'s to say.
- **The table lists a line once.** Lines the sections above already show (what was held to the task file, what git
  showed) are counted in the table and marked as listed above; a claim's verdict, an entry in the owner's name, and
  a record's broken chain are shown in the table itself. `--json` carries every group whole.

Held by `crates/av-record/src/groups_tests.rs`, with a record that puts a line under each group and one that does
not, and by `crates/av-cli/tests/report.rs`. With a person's commits read as the tool's, with a claim the record
cannot show called contradicted, and with an empty group saying nothing, tests fail each time (one, one, and two).

With this, backlog 0715 is done. Of the design's six groups, five have a record behind them and one waits for the
pass-through, which is also what `[may] reach` and `ask-first` wait for.

### Part 6, 21 November 2026: the pass-through (decision 10; backlog 0716, part 1)

`av proxy [--app <folder>] -- <the tool server's command>`, in `crates/av-cli/src/proxy.rs`, with what its record
keeps in `crates/av-record/src/passthrough.rs`. It is put in front of a tool server's command in an agent's settings;
it starts that server, relays every line each way, and writes down each call to a tool (`tools/call`) and how it was
answered. `av report` does not read the record yet: that is the item's part 2.

- **It changes nothing it relays.** Every byte goes on as it came, whether or not `av` could read it: a line that is
  not a message, is not text, or is longer than 4 MB is relayed and not recorded. Held end to end by a test whose
  server sends an answer's text and bytes that are not text back through it.
- **What a line of the record holds.** The time by `av`'s clock; the tool's name; how the call was answered (`ok`;
  `tool-error`, when the tool ran and said it failed; `refused`, with the server's own error number, when the server
  answered with an error and the call was not carried out; `unanswered`, when the pass-through ended first); a
  SHA-256 of the arguments and their size; and a summary of the arguments, at most 200 characters, with any key cut
  as the secrets scan cuts one. **The summary is the owner's choice, 21 November 2026** ("redacted summary too"),
  over a line with the fingerprint and size alone: arguments can hold file contents and the person's own words, and
  the summary is what lets a report say what was tried again. Never an answer's text, and never the arguments whole.
- **Where the record is kept.** Beside the hook records, in `proxy-records` under `sv`'s state folder, one file per
  app named by a hash of the app's full path, readable only by the person, chained as the hook record's lines are.
  Nothing is written into the app's folder. The app is `--app`, or the folder `av proxy` was started in.
- **It never stops the agent's work.** A record that cannot be written is said once on standard error, counted, and
  the relaying goes on; the count is said when the server ends.
- **Its own options end at `--`.** What follows is the server's command and is never read as `av`'s. `av proxy` ends
  with the server's exit code, or 3 when the server could not be started.

**This changes two things decision 10 and part 2 said of `av`, and both are meant.** `av` now starts a program: the
one named on `av proxy`'s command line, and no other, in one source file, which the test of `av`'s own source holds
(`starts_a_program == ["proxy.rs"]`). And `av` now writes a file: the pass-through's record, outside the app's
folder. It still opens no network connection of its own; whatever the server it starts connects to is the server's,
as it was before `av` was put in front of it.

Putting `av proxy` in an agent's settings is the consent to this record, as installing the hook is for the hook
record (ADR-122); reading it into a report will ask each time, as the hook record does.

Not built here: a tool server reached over HTTP is not passed through, only one started as a command; and the chain
has no key yet (part 3 of the item, with the owner's choice of two keys recorded there).

### Part 7, 22 November 2026: checkpoints (decision 12; backlog 0716, part 3, the first half)

`av checkpoint <folder>`, in `crates/av-cli/src/checkpoint.rs`, with what a checkpoint is in
`crates/av-record/src/checkpoint.rs`. The owner, at their own terminal, signs where each record of an app has got to;
a later `av report` says whether the lines signed for are still as they were.

**Two keys, two jobs: the owner's choice, 22 November 2026** ("agree with your two-key proposal"). The pass-through is
started by the agent's own tooling with nobody at a keyboard, so it cannot use a key that has a passphrase; and any
key it can read by itself, the agent can read too. So the chain keeps a key of its own for what a key can do there
(the second half of this part, not built yet), and the owner's signing key, the one `sv review` uses, is used only
when the owner runs `av checkpoint`.

- **What is signed.** For each of the three records (the build-loop record, the hook record, the pass-through's), how
  many chained lines it holds and its chain's last link, with the record's kind, for this app. Signing reads where a
  chain ends, never what a record holds. The signature is one of `sv review`'s (`v3:`, ADR-043), so it names the app
  and is checked against the keys this computer trusts for it.
- **`av` never makes a key and never keeps a passphrase.** With no signing key, it says `sv review` makes one and
  signs nothing. A passphrase is typed without being shown, used once, and asked for at most three times. A key with
  no passphrase signs, and `av checkpoint` and the later report both say that anything running as the owner could
  have made that checkpoint.
- **Only at a terminal.** `av checkpoint` refuses when its input is not a terminal. As ADR-043 says of `sv review`,
  a program can stand in for a terminal; the passphrase is what a program does not have.
- **A record whose chain does not check out is not signed:** that would be signing for a record already changed.
- **Where checkpoints are kept.** One file per app beside the other records, outside the app's folder, a line per
  checkpoint: the time, the record, the line count, the link, the signature, and the key's fingerprint as the signer
  said it. The report takes the key from the signature, never from that last field.
- **What a report says.** For each record it read: no checkpoint (every line rests on the chain alone, which a tool
  running as the owner could make again); the first N lines stand as signed, by which key and what this computer
  knows of it, and lines after them rest on the chain alone; changed since the checkpoint; or a checkpoint that could
  not be taken as the owner's, and why, read as no checkpoint. A record changed since its checkpoint is a line under
  "Changes to what watches the tool".
- **What a checkpoint shows that a chain cannot:** a record replaced whole, with a chain made again from the start.
  The new record checks out by itself, and does not pass through what was signed. Held by tests at each level.
- **Consent is unchanged.** The hook record is set against its checkpoints only for a run given `--hook-record`; the
  pass-through's record is not read by `av report` yet, and the report says so for each.

The helper that keeps a typed passphrase off the screen moved from `sv-cli`'s `review.rs` into
`av_record::plain::quiet`, word for word, so there is one of it; `sv review` reads it from there.

Held by `crates/av-record/src/checkpoint_tests.rs`, `crates/av-cli/src/checkpoint_tests.rs`, and
`crates/av-cli/tests/report.rs`. With a record always taken to pass through its checkpoint, two tests fail; with a
checkpoint taken as the owner's whoever signed it, one fails.

Not built: the chain's own key (the second half of this part). And nothing here stops a checkpoint from being
deleted: a record with no checkpoint reads as one never checkpointed, which the report states plainly but cannot tell
from a checkpoint removed.

### Part 8, 22 November 2026: a note kept outside the folder, in place of a key for the chain (decision 12, changed; backlog 0716, part 3, the second half)

**Decision 12 said "the chain that joins a record's lines takes a key". It does not, at the owner's word, 22 November
2026: "go with the note outside the folder".** Asked to choose between the key as written, a note kept outside the
app's folder, and neither, with this account of each.

What a key for the chain would have guarded: one case only. The build-loop record is the one record kept inside the
app's folder; a tool kept to that folder can rewrite it and make its chain again, between the owner's checkpoints.
The hook record and the pass-through's record are outside the folder, so such a tool cannot touch them, and a tool
that is not kept to the folder could read a key as easily as a record.

What the key would have cost: every line of `sv`'s records written another way, with older records still to be
checked the old way; a new "could not be checked" state in `sv`'s own report wherever the key was missing; and a
record rewritten whole in the old way would have read as one from before there was a key.

What is built instead (`crates/av-record/src/outside_note.rs`): each time `sv`'s MCP server writes a line of the
build-loop record, it also writes down, outside the app's folder, where that record's chain has got to: the time, how
many chained lines, and the chain's last link, and nothing of what the record holds. `av report` then looks at
whether the record still passes through every place noted, and says one of: nothing was noted; it passes through
every place noted, with how many lines came after the last note; or it changed since a note, which is also a line
under "Changes to what watches the tool". A record rewritten whole inside the folder, with a chain of its own that
checks out, does not pass.

- **No line of the record changes,** so nothing about how `sv` reads or reports its record changes, and `sv report`
  says what it said. Only `av report` reads the notes, for now.
- **Only the server a person starts writes notes** (`outside_note::ON`, turned on by `sv mcp` once it is about to
  serve, and only when the record itself is on). A test that writes a record writes no note into the folder of
  whoever runs the tests.
- **A note is never an error.** One that cannot be written is one place less noted; the report shows the notes
  stopping short, as lines that came after the last note.
- **A record whose chain does not check out is not noted:** the note would vouch for the change.
- **Its limit, said every time it is shown:** a tool that can write anywhere the owner can, can change the notes too.
  Only a checkpoint the owner signed shows a change made by such a tool, and only to lines signed for. And where
  `sv`'s MCP server runs through Docker without its records folder, it can note nothing, and the report says nothing
  was noted.

Held by `crates/av-record/src/outside_note_tests.rs`, by `crates/sv-cli/tests/note_outside.rs`, which starts `sv mcp`
itself, and by `crates/av-cli/tests/report.rs`. With a record always taken to pass through what was noted, two tests
and one fail; with `sv mcp` not turning notes on, one fails.

With this, backlog 0716's part 3 is done. Its part 2, the report reading the pass-through's record, is what is left.
