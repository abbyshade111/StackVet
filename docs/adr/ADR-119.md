# ADR-119: A probe reads its answer against its own setup and its own control

**Status:** accepted, 15 October 2026, in the pull request that builds the first sweep (backlog 0559, by session
honesty). Proposed on 14 October in the backlog claim that files the three sweeps (the owner's "agree, let's make
those pattern fixes", the same day, after the three-tool prompt trial,
`docs/research/SETUP-PROMPT-TRIALS-2026-10-14.md`), written by the orchestrating session.

**Governs:** `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/signin.rs`, `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/uploads.rs`,
`crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/limit.rs`, `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/forgery.rs`, `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/admin.rs`,
`crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/codes.rs`, `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/control.rs`,
and every other probe file in the signed_in folder of sv-check that makes an account, a record, or a control request
of its own. A pull request that
changes how a probe reads an answer it compares with its setup or its control changes this record too, or says in its
description why it does not (`docs/adr/README.md`).

## Context

The three-tool prompt trial of 14 October 2026 ran `sv` on three apps built from one brief. Four of the ten "needs
attention" lines across the three reports were `sv`'s own faults, and three of them had one shape: the probe's bar was
right, and its reading of the answer was wrong.

- The guessing-limit probe signed up an account of its own, never read the answer (403: an invite code was needed),
  guessed seven times at an address with no account, and wrote a high finding about "an account this check made for
  it" (backlog 0522). The same report's gaps section said the sign-up had failed.
- The browser-validation probe sent a value breaking the form's own rule and read any status under 400 as accepted;
  the control sign-up had answered 302 and the broken one 200 with an error on the page (backlog 0523).
- The lockout probe stopped at the push-back after five wrong passwords and credited the limit in part, holding the
  account's real password and never trying it; the app verified every guess and let the right one in (backlog 0524).

CLAUDE.md already says a test whose setup can fail quietly must first assert the setup worked. The probes are tests of
somebody else's app, and the rule was not written down for them.

## Decision

Three rules for every probe under `signed_in/`, built as three sweeps across all of them, not one item at a time:

1. **Setup is asserted before anything is concluded.** A probe that makes an account, a record, or a session of its own
   reads the answer that made it, and compares it with the control the run already has (the ordinary sign-up, the
   ordinary create). When the setup did not work, the probe reports its requirements as not assessed, with the reason
   in plain words (what was sent, what answered, what the manifest may be missing), and never a finding. The wording
   "an account this check made for it" is used only when the account was made.
2. **An answer is judged against the control, not against a status threshold alone.** Accepted means "answered the way
   the control did": the same status class, the same redirect target or page shape. An answer that differs from the
   control in a way the probe cannot read ("200 where the control redirected") is "could not tell", and the step says
   what was compared. A second request that would settle it (signing in as the account the broken value would have
   made, reading the record back) is made when the manifest allows it.
3. **A probe that holds the evidence to finish sends the one more request.** After a lockout's push-back, the real
   password is tried once: if it signs in, the limit is cosmetic, and the probe reports it and credits nothing. The
   same rule names the anonymous anti-forgery token (fetched twice as two strangers and compared) and any probe that
   stops one request short of what its requirement asks.

Each sweep is built with a fake-app case per probe that breaks the rule and watches the credit or finding go red
before the fix, as CLAUDE.md asks.

## Consequences

- Fewer wrong findings, and each "not assessed" line says what the person can change in the manifest.
- A run makes a few more requests (one per lockout probe, two per anonymous-token check, one per validation probe
  that could not tell); all inside the fence, against `sv`'s own copy of the app.
- A probe's credit can only get stricter under these rules; nothing that was not credited before becomes credited.
- Backlog 0522, 0523, and 0524 are the first cases of sweeps 1, 2, and 3 and close with them.

## Later

**15 October 2026: the first sweep, built (backlog 0559).** Every check under `signed_in/` that makes an account of
its own through `signup` was read for whether it shows the account was made before concluding anything:
- **Asserted now:** the password-guessing check (V6.3.1; backlog 0522) signs in as its account and opens the first
  private page before a wrong password is sent, and without that says the question is not assessed, what the sign-up
  answered, and that the form may need a value stackvet.toml does not carry, such as an invite code. The emailed
  code's first check does the same before asking for a code; the later emailed-code checks read what it said rather
  than signing in again.
- **Already asserted, through `passwords::account_works` or a sign-in that opens a private page:** the password rules,
  the password change, the email change, re-sign-up, account deletion, the reset, the role written into sign-up, the
  role written into the email change, the changes beside the email change (ADR-105), and the log marker's successful
  sign-in.
- **Safe without it, since they are only ever a finding and an account never made answers as the address with no
  account does:** whether a failed sign-in, or a sign-up, tells accounts apart.
- **Records and uploads, already asserted by a control:** the cross-site and simple-request checks (0420, ADR-118),
  the burst (its first create), and the uploads (an ordinary file first).

Each check that signs in to show its account was made adds a sign-in, so the spec's number is 62, not 60 (ADR-028,
Later, 15 October 2026).

**15 October 2026: the second sweep, its first batch (backlog 0560).** Rule 2's comparison is one function,
`signed_in/control.rs`: an answer is like the control's when it has the same status class, the same redirect target
(its path alone), and, for two successes, JSON with the same top-level names, or no form where the control's page had
none; a 4xx where the control was not refused is a refusal; anything else is "could not tell", in words that say what
was compared. The browser-validation probe (0523) reads its answer that way, and refines the rule's "when the
manifest allows it": with a private page listed, it signs in as the account the broken value would have made whether
the answer looked like the control's or not, since two sign-ups sent back to the form alike made nothing; with none, an
answer like the control's is still a finding and one unlike it is not assessed. The inventory of every other read is
in 0560, and each is built as a batch of its own.

**15 October 2026: the second sweep, its second batch (backlog 0560).** The cross-site and simple-request checks
(V3.5.1, V3.5.2) settle a forged create by reading its record back, as rule 2's second request: the record holding
the forged marker is a request taken, and the record not there, read the way that found the control's own record, is
a request that made nothing. A way of reading that did not find the control's record (a create with no `{marker}`, a
page cut short) is not believed, and the answer is read beside the control's instead. Redirects are compared by the
shape of their path, a part holding a digit read as an id, so `/notes/7` and `/notes/8` are one place.

**15 October 2026: the second sweep, its third batch (backlog 0560).** The upload checks (V5.2.1, V5.2.2, and V5.2.3
for archives) read an answer beside the ordinary file's, and settle it, where `serves-at` found the ordinary GIF by
its name, by fetching the file back by its own name. The archive is not fetched back, since an app may unpack it
rather than keep it as sent. The refusal-stands file sent after a refusal must be answered as the first ordinary GIF
was before the refusal counts.

**15 October 2026: from the third sweep's inventory, item 8 (backlog 0571).** The download-name check (V5.4.2) sends a

**15 October 2026: the wrong-password check with no sign-up tries a right password too (backlog 0572).** With no
`signup`, the check guesses at a name no account has, so after a push-back A signs in once, from the same address.
Refused, the credit says so: a limit by address that stops the right password too. Let in, the credit is withheld and
no finding is made, though the backlog item asked for a finding: an app that counts wrong passwords for each name, a
sound design, lets A in just as a limit that refuses only wrong passwords does, and with no account of its own the
check cannot tell the two apart. The report says so and asks for a `signup` section, as it already did where the app
did not push back at all (backlog 0384).

**15 October 2026: the burst's limit tried in a fresh session (backlog 0573).** Where the burst (V2.4.1) would credit a
limit, its last create refused as a limit refuses, the second user signs in again, in a session of its own, and sends
one more create inside the same minute, before the burst's limit is left to lapse. Refused as a limit refuses, the
credit stands and says so. Taken, the limit counts each session, not the user, so one user signing in again goes on
creating: not credited and not a finding, since the burst alone did not show records going through without limit.
Not signed in, or answered otherwise, nothing is credited. This changes what counts as evidence for V2.4.1: a limit
refusing the end of one session's burst is no longer enough. The sign-in is spent only where a credit is at stake, and
the new session takes the old one's place, as an app keeping one session a user would have it.
