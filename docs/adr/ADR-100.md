# ADR-100: `sv report` says how long its slow stages take, how far a long wait is, and the slowest parts, unless `--quiet`

**Status:** accepted, 12 October 2026, in the pull request that builds it. The owner's word on backlog 0247, passed on by
the session organizing the backlog the same day: all three parts, printed "regardless of what tool someone is using",
after a quiet terminal while running `sv` from Cursor; and a `--quiet` that silences only progress and timing lines.

**Governs:** `crates/sv-cli/src/assemble.rs` (the time said after a slow stage, and `--quiet`), `crates/sv-cli/src/main.rs`
(where `sv report` says them, and the slowest parts at its end), `crates/sv-check/src/signed_in/sessions.rs` (the line a
long `--slow` wait says as it goes) and `crates/sv-run/src/docker.rs` (where the run passes that line to the terminal). A
pull request that changes these changes this record too, or says in its description why it does not
(`docs/adr/README.md`).

## Context

`sv report` already said each of its ten stages as it began, and a line as each outside tool or suite began, and every
report page names the five slowest parts at its top. What a person watching could not see: how long a slow stage took,
the slowest parts without opening the report, and anything at all during `--slow`'s waits, which wait out the app's
session timeouts and can last an hour and a half. In the Cursor build the owner asked whether that wait had stalled.

## Decision

- **A stage that took five seconds or more says so when it ends,** as "sv report: 9 of 10 took 48 s".
- **The slowest parts are said at the end** of `sv report`, the same line the report pages carry.
- **A long `--slow` wait says how far it is** at each step, as "now: waiting out the session timeouts: 4 of 16
  minutes". The check gives the line to the run, and the run prints it; a test's fake app prints nothing.
- **`sv report --quiet` leaves out the progress and timing lines,** the stage, step, waiting and slowest-parts lines.
  Counts, warnings and errors are never left out by it, and the report is the same either way.
- All of it goes to the terminal's error stream, as the stage lines do, so the report on standard output is unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave it to the AI coding tool.** A tool that holds a command's output until it ends, or does not show MCP progress,
  would still show nothing; this makes the terminal say it whatever the tool does.
- **A line every few seconds during every stage.** Noisy; the step lines already show each suite and tool as it begins.

## Consequences

- A long run reads as moving at every stage; a short one adds at most the slowest-parts line.
- The MCP server's progress notifications are unchanged.
